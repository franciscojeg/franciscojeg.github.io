0. Before you start: what this exam looks like
CLF-C02 is AWS's broadest foundational exam in terms of service coverage, but also its most conceptual one. You are not expected to configure anything: you are expected to recognize what each service category does, how the shared responsibility model works, how the cloud gets billed, and where to find help. The ideal candidate has up to six months of AWS exposure, in any role, technical or not.

1. Cloud Concepts 24%
The "why" of the cloud: what cloud computing is, what benefits it brings over on-premises infrastructure, and AWS's official frameworks for adopting and operating it well (CAF, Well-Architected, migration strategies).

1.1 What cloud computing is and its benefits

BenefitIn plain words
Trade capital expense for variable expenseYou pay for what you use instead of investing large sums upfront in data centers and infrastructure.
Benefit from massive economies of scaleBy aggregating usage from thousands of customers, AWS achieves lower prices than any single company running its own infrastructure.
Stop guessing capacityYou scale up or down based on actual demand, instead of over-provisioning "just in case" or under-provisioning and falling short.
Increase speed and agilityResources are available in minutes, which speeds up experimentation and lowers the cost of trying new ideas.
Stop spending money running and maintaining data centersYou focus time and money on what differentiates your business, not on operating servers.
Go global in minutesYou deploy applications across multiple regions worldwide with a few clicks, bringing the application closer to end users.
Exam hint: if the scenario contrasts "buying servers upfront" against "paying only for what you use," it almost always points to the variable expense benefit or to no longer investing in your own infrastructure (CapEx to OpEx).

1.2 AWS Cloud Adoption Framework (AWS CAF)

The CAF organizes the transformation toward the cloud into six perspectives. They are not sequential steps: they are areas of the organization that need to advance together.

PerspectiveFocus
BusinessEnsures cloud investments accelerate business outcomes and digital transformation.
PeopleEvolves the organization's culture and capabilities toward continuous learning in the cloud.
GovernanceOrchestrates cloud initiatives while maximizing organizational benefit and minimizing risk.
PlatformBuilds a scalable hybrid platform and modernizes existing workloads.
SecurityAchieves confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and cloud workloads.
OperationsEnsures cloud services are delivered at the level the business needs.

1.3 AWS Well-Architected Framework

Six pillars for evaluating whether an architecture is well built. The exam expects you to recognize which pillar sits behind each design concern, not to apply the practices technically.

PillarQuestion it answers
Operational ExcellenceCan I run and monitor the system, and keep improving processes over time?
SecurityHow do I protect information and systems from unauthorized access?
ReliabilityDoes the system recover quickly from failures and perform its function consistently?
Performance EfficiencyAm I using compute resources as efficiently as possible?
Cost OptimizationAm I delivering business value at the lowest possible price point?
SustainabilityAm I minimizing the environmental impact and energy consumption of my workloads?
Common trap: "sustainability" is the newest pillar and the easiest one to forget. If the scenario mentions reducing carbon footprint or energy consumption, that's this pillar, not cost optimization.

1.4 Migration strategies (the 7 R's)

StrategyWhat it involves
Rehost (lift and shift)Move the application to the cloud as-is, with no changes. The fastest one to execute.
Replatform (lift, tinker, and shift)Move with a few targeted optimizations, such as switching to a managed database, without redesigning the app.
Repurchase (drop and shop)Replace the application with a different product or version, typically moving from traditional licenses to a SaaS model.
Refactor / Re-architectRedesign the application to take advantage of cloud-native capabilities. The highest-effort option, not recommended for large migrations due to its complexity.
RetireDecommission applications that no longer provide business value.
RetainKeep the application in its current environment or postpone its migration.
RelocateMove servers or instances to a cloud version of the same platform, a different VPC, region, or account, with minimal change.
For large migrations, AWS Prescriptive Guidance recommends prioritizing rehost, replatform, relocate, and retire, leaving refactor for after the migration is complete, once the team has experience operating in the cloud.
2. Security and Compliance 30%
The second-heaviest domain. It covers who is responsible for what in the cloud, how access is managed, and which services exist to govern, audit, and protect an AWS account.

2.1 Shared responsibility model

Who's responsibleWhat they handle
AWS: security "of" the cloudHardware, physical facilities, networking, and virtualization that underpin every service. Host operating systems and the virtualization layer.
Customer: security "in" the cloudDepends on the service chosen. For IaaS services like EC2, the customer manages the guest operating system, patches, application software, and firewall configuration (security groups).
Exam hint: the higher a service's abstraction level (from EC2 toward S3 or DynamoDB), the more responsibility AWS takes on and the less the customer manages. The customer is always responsible for classifying their data, encrypting it where required, and managing access permissions (IAM), regardless of the service.

2.2 AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM)

ConceptWhat it is
Root userThe identity created when the account is opened, with full access. AWS recommends not using it for daily tasks and protecting it with MFA.
IAM userAn individual identity within the account, with its own credentials, for a person or application.
IAM groupA set of users who share the same permissions, to manage access more simply.
IAM roleA temporary identity that a user, service, or application can assume, without long-term credentials.
PolicyA JSON document that defines which actions are allowed or denied on which resources.
MFAMulti-factor authentication: a second verification factor in addition to the password.
AWS IAM Identity CenterCentralized access management across multiple AWS accounts and applications, with single sign-on (SSO).
Common trap: the "principle of least privilege" means granting only the permissions strictly needed, never broad access "just in case." It's one of the most tested concepts in this domain.

2.3 Compliance, audit, and governance

ServiceWhat it's for
AWS ArtifactSelf-service portal for downloading AWS compliance reports (SOC, ISO) and accepting agreements such as the HIPAA BAA.
AWS Audit ManagerContinuously collects evidence to simplify how risk and regulatory compliance are assessed.
AWS ConfigRecords and evaluates resource configuration over time, and flags deviations from defined rules.
AWS CloudTrailRecords every API call made in the account: who did what, when, and from where. The account's core audit service.
AWS OrganizationsCentrally manages multiple AWS accounts, with service control policies (SCPs) and consolidated billing.
AWS Control TowerAutomates setting up a multi-account environment with governance best practices already applied.

2.4 Specialized security services

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon GuardDutyIntelligent threat detection that analyzes logs for malicious or unauthorized activity.
Amazon InspectorAutomated vulnerability scanning for workloads, such as EC2 instances and container images.
Amazon MacieUses machine learning to discover and classify sensitive data (such as personal information) stored in Amazon S3.
AWS Key Management Service (KMS)Creates and controls the encryption keys used to protect data at rest.
AWS Secrets ManagerStores and rotates credentials, passwords, and API keys securely.
AWS ShieldManaged protection against distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks.
AWS WAFWeb application firewall that filters malicious traffic headed to CloudFront, API Gateway, or a load balancer.
AWS Security HubConsolidates and prioritizes security alerts from multiple AWS services in a single place.
3. Cloud Technology and Services 34%
The heaviest domain. It tests whether you recognize what each category of AWS services does and when to use it, without needing to know how to configure it.

3.1 Ways to interact with AWS

MethodWhen it's used
AWS Management ConsoleGraphical web interface, great for exploring services and one-off tasks.
AWS CLICommand line for automating tasks and scripting against any AWS service.
AWS SDKCode libraries for integrating AWS services directly into an application, across several programming languages.
Infrastructure as Code (IaC)Defining and deploying infrastructure through versionable templates instead of manual steps, for example with AWS CloudFormation.

3.2 AWS global infrastructure

ConceptWhat it is
RegionAn independent geographic location with multiple data centers, chosen for proximity to users, regulatory compliance, or service availability.
Availability Zone (AZ)One or more discrete data centers within a region, with independent power, cooling, and networking, for fault tolerance.
Edge locationA point of presence used by Amazon CloudFront and Route 53 to bring content and DNS resolution closer to the end user.
AWS Local Zones and OutpostsExtend AWS infrastructure closer to major cities or directly inside the customer's own data center.
Exam hint: if the scenario asks for high availability within a single region, the answer is usually deploying across multiple Availability Zones. If it asks for proximity to users in different countries, the answer is usually multiple regions or CloudFront.

3.3 Compute

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon EC2Resizable virtual servers with full control over the operating system. The foundation of compute on AWS.
AWS LambdaRuns code without provisioning servers, billing only for execution time. The archetypal serverless service.
AWS Elastic BeanstalkDeploys and manages web applications automatically, without managing the underlying infrastructure in detail.
Amazon LightsailSimplified virtual servers with fixed monthly pricing, aimed at small projects and people new to the cloud.
AWS OutpostsBrings AWS infrastructure and services into the customer's own data center.
AWS BatchRuns batch processing jobs at any scale, automatically managing the underlying compute provisioning.

3.4 Containers

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon ECSAWS's own container orchestrator, simple to operate within the AWS ecosystem.
Amazon EKSAWS-managed Kubernetes, for teams that already use, or want to use, the industry standard.
Amazon ECRManaged registry for storing and distributing container images.
AWS FargateServerless compute engine for containers, usable with ECS or EKS, without managing underlying EC2 instances.

3.5 Storage

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon S3Object storage with very high durability, for any type and volume of file.
Amazon S3 GlacierS3 storage classes for long-term archiving, with different retrieval times and much lower cost.
Amazon EBSPersistent block storage volumes that attach to an EC2 instance, like a virtual hard drive.
Amazon EFSManaged, shared file system, mountable simultaneously from multiple EC2 instances.
Amazon FSxManaged third-party file systems (Windows File Server, Lustre, NetApp ONTAP), for specific workloads.
AWS Storage GatewayConnects on-premises environments with cloud storage, acting as a hybrid gateway.
AWS BackupCentralizes and automates backups across different AWS services from a single place.
S3 storage classWhen to use it
S3 StandardFrequently accessed, general-purpose data: websites, applications, analytics.
S3 Intelligent-TieringWhen the access pattern is unpredictable: automatically moves data between cost tiers.
S3 Standard-IA / One Zone-IAInfrequently accessed data that still needs millisecond availability, such as backups.
S3 Glacier Instant RetrievalLong-term archive accessed a few times a year, but needing instant retrieval.
S3 Glacier Flexible RetrievalLow-cost backup and archive, with retrieval in minutes to hours.
S3 Glacier Deep ArchiveThe most economical option, for data almost never accessed, with a 12 to 48 hour retrieval time.

3.6 Databases

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon RDSManaged relational databases (MySQL, PostgreSQL, SQL Server, among others), with automated patching and backups.
Amazon AuroraRelational database compatible with MySQL and PostgreSQL, built by AWS for higher performance and availability.
Amazon DynamoDBFully managed NoSQL key-value and document database, with millisecond latency at any scale.
Amazon ElastiCacheManaged in-memory cache, used to speed up applications by reducing load on the primary database.
Amazon NeptuneManaged graph database, for highly connected relationships such as social networks or recommendation engines.
Amazon DocumentDBMongoDB-compatible document database.

3.7 Networking and content delivery

ServiceWhat it's for
Amazon VPCAn isolated, private virtual network within AWS, where resources are deployed.
Amazon Route 53Managed DNS service, also used for traffic routing and health checks.
Amazon CloudFrontContent delivery network (CDN) that distributes content from edge locations close to the end user.
AWS Direct ConnectDedicated, private network connection between an on-premises data center and AWS, bypassing the public internet.
AWS VPN (Site-to-Site and Client VPN)Encrypted connection over the public internet between an on-premises network or an individual user and AWS.
AWS Transit GatewayCentral hub for connecting multiple VPCs and on-premises networks, simplifying network topology.
Elastic Load Balancing (ELB)Automatically distributes incoming traffic across multiple instances or services for higher availability.
AWS Global AcceleratorImproves the performance and availability of global applications by using AWS's private network instead of the public internet.
Amazon API GatewayCreates, publishes, and manages APIs at any scale, acting as the entry point to backend services.

3.8 Application integration and analytics

3.10 Management, monitoring, and governance

4. Billing, Pricing, and Support 12%
The smallest domain by weight, but a very concrete one: pricing models, tools for estimating and controlling costs, and which support plan fits which type of customer.

4.1 AWS pricing models

ModelWhen it fits
On-DemandPay by the hour or second, with no commitment. Ideal for unpredictable or short-lived workloads.
Reserved InstancesDiscounts of up to 72% versus On-Demand in exchange for committing to steady usage for 1 or 3 years.
Savings PlansA discount similar to Reserved Instances, but committing to an hourly spend amount instead of a specific instance type, offering more flexibility.
Spot InstancesUses AWS's spare capacity at discounts of up to 90%, but can be interrupted by AWS with little notice. Ideal for fault-tolerant workloads.
Common trap: Spot is the cheapest but least reliable option (it can be interrupted). Reserved and Savings Plans require a time commitment but guarantee capacity. On-Demand requires no commitment but is the most expensive option per hour.

4.2 Estimating and controlling costs

ToolWhat it's for
AWS Pricing CalculatorEstimates the cost of an architecture before deploying it, by adding up the planned services.
AWS Cost ExplorerVisualizes and analyzes spending and usage patterns over time, with forecasts.
AWS BudgetsSets budgets and sends alerts when spend or usage approaches or exceeds a defined limit.
AWS Cost and Usage ReportsThe most detailed and granular report available on account cost and usage.
AWS Organizations (consolidated billing)Combines billing for several accounts into a single bill, and lets volume discounts be shared across them.

4.3 AWS Support plans

PlanWho it's for
Basic SupportIncluded at no extra cost with every AWS account: access to documentation, forums, service health checks, and account and billing support.
Business Support+24/7 access to support engineers by chat, phone, and web, with 500+ AWS Trusted Advisor checks and a response time under 30 minutes for business-critical cases. Built for production workloads.
Enterprise SupportEverything above, plus a dedicated Technical Account Manager (TAM), a response time of up to 15 minutes for critical cases, and strategic reviews with AWS experts. Built for organizations running mission-critical workloads.
A note on plan names: the official CLF-C02 exam guide still lists the historical plan names (Developer Support, Business Support, and Enterprise On-Ramp Support, alongside Enterprise Support). AWS has announced that Developer Support and Business Support will retire on January 1, 2027 in favor of a single plan called Business Support+, and that Enterprise On-Ramp automatically moves to Enterprise Support at contract renewal. If your exam still asks about the older names, treat them as an earlier version of Business Support+ and Enterprise Support respectively: the logic of "who needs which plan" doesn't change.

4.4 Other support resources and marketplace

ResourceWhat it's for

AWS Knowledge CenterOfficial answers to the most frequently asked support questions.
AWS MarketplaceDigital catalog of third-party software ready to run on AWS, with centralized cost, governance, and entitlement management.
AWS Professional Services and Solutions ArchitectsDirect expert assistance from AWS to design, plan, and implement cloud projects.
AWS Partner Network (APN)Independent software vendors and systems integrators certified by AWS, with training benefits and volume discounts.

Out of scope

You are not expected to recognize or configure: niche or highly specialized services such as game tech (Amazon GameLift), media services (the AWS Elemental family), robotics (AWS RoboMaker), advanced developer tools (AWS CodeArtifact, AWS CodeDeploy, Amazon CodeGuru), specialized databases (Amazon Keyspaces, Amazon MemoryDB), and more advanced networking services (AWS Cloud Map, AWS Network Firewall, Amazon VPC Lattice). You're also not expected to write code, design architectures, troubleshoot technical issues, implement solutions, or run load tests.
6. Comparisons that decide questions
PairQuick call
Region vs. Availability ZoneA Region is the full geographic location. An AZ is one independent data center within that Region.
S3 vs. EBS vs. EFSS3 stores objects accessible over the internet. EBS is a disk for a single EC2 instance. EFS is a file system shared across multiple instances.
RDS vs. DynamoDBRDS is relational (tables with a fixed schema). DynamoDB is NoSQL (key-value or document, no fixed schema).
On-Demand vs. Reserved vs. Savings Plans vs. SpotNo commitment and expensive per hour vs. instance commitment vs. flexible spend commitment vs. cheap but interruptible spare capacity.
CloudTrail vs. CloudWatchCloudTrail records WHO did WHAT (API auditing). CloudWatch monitors PERFORMANCE and HEALTH (metrics, logs, alarms).
AWS Config vs. CloudTrailConfig tracks HOW a resource's configuration changes over time. CloudTrail records EVERY API call.
Direct Connect vs. Site-to-Site VPNDirect Connect is a dedicated, private physical connection. VPN is an encrypted connection over the public internet, faster to set up.
AWS's responsibility vs. the customer'sAWS secures the physical infrastructure and virtualization. The customer is always responsible for their data and for managing access (IAM), no matter the service.

8. Final checklist before the exam
  • I can name the six benefits of the cloud and give an example of each.
  • I can name the six AWS CAF perspectives and the six Well-Architected pillars without mixing them up.
  • I can explain the 7 R's of migration with an example of each.
  • I can explain the shared responsibility model and how it shifts by service (EC2 versus S3).
  • I can tell apart an IAM user, group, role, and policy.
  • I can name at least three representative services for each category in the official guide (analytics, compute, database, networking, security, storage, among others).
  • I can explain the difference between On-Demand, Reserved, Savings Plans, and Spot.
  • I can explain which support plan fits a critical production workload versus a personal account.
  • I know the official guide uses support plan names that AWS is migrating to Business Support+ and Enterprise Support.
