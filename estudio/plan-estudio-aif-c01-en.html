Week-by-week plan
Week 1 · D1: Fundamentals of AI and ML (from zero)
D1 · 20%
Core terminology and concepts
AI vs Machine Learning vs Deep Learning: hierarchy and differences. Neural networks, computer vision, natural language processing (NLP), models, algorithms, training and inference, bias, fairness, fit, and large language models (LLM). Inference types: batch or real-time. Data types: labeled and unlabeled, tabular, time series, images, text, structured and unstructured.
D1 · 20%
Learning types and AI use cases
Supervised learning (classification, regression), unsupervised learning (clustering, anomaly detection), and reinforcement learning. When AI adds value (scalability, automation, decision support) and when it isn't the right fit (cost-benefit analysis, deterministic outcomes required). Real-world examples: computer vision, NLP, speech recognition, recommendation systems, fraud detection, and forecasting.
D1 · 20%
AWS managed AI and machine learning services
Full catalog: Amazon SageMaker, Rekognition, Textract, Transcribe, Translate, Polly, Lex, Kendra, Comprehend, Personalize, Forecast, and Fraud Detector. Memorize each service's "job title" before diving into technical detail.
D1 · 20%
Machine learning development lifecycle
Pipeline components: data collection, exploratory data analysis (EDA), preprocessing, feature engineering, training, hyperparameter tuning, evaluation, deployment, and monitoring. Model sources: open-source pretrained models or custom training. MLOps fundamentals. Performance metrics (accuracy, AUC, F1) versus business metrics (cost per user, ROI).
Day 1-2
AI/ML/DL terminology + learning types (supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement) + data and inference types
4h · conceptual foundation for the whole exam, don't move on if something isn't clear
Day 3-4
AI use cases + AWS managed services catalog
4h · 12 services, start an Anki card per service today
Day 5-6
ML lifecycle, MLOps, and evaluation metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC-ROC)
4h · tell model metrics apart from business metrics
Day 7
Week 1 review + 25-question D1 mini quiz
2h · 20% of the exam rides on this week
If this is your first exposure to AI or AWS, this week sets the pace for the next four. Don't move on to Domain 2 without being clear on the difference between supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning. Don't move on either without recognizing the "job title" of each managed service.
Week 2 · D2: Fundamentals of Generative AI
D2 · 24%
Core generative AI concepts
Tokens, chunking, embeddings, vectors, prompt engineering, transformer-based LLMs, foundation models, multimodal models, and diffusion models. Foundation model lifecycle: data selection, model selection, pre-training, fine-tuning, evaluation, deployment, and feedback.
D2 · 24%
Generative AI use cases
Image, video, and audio generation. Text summarization, chatbots, translation, code generation, customer service agents, search engines, and recommendation systems.
D2 · 24%
Capabilities, limitations, and model selection
Advantages of generative AI: adaptability, responsiveness, and simplicity. Drawbacks: hallucinations, interpretability, inaccuracy, and non-determinism. Model selection factors: model type, performance requirements, capabilities, constraints, and compliance. Business value: cross-domain performance, efficiency, conversion rate, average revenue per user, and customer lifetime value.
D2 · 24%
AWS infrastructure and technologies for generative AI
Amazon Bedrock (managed API for foundation models), SageMaker JumpStart (fine-tuning and deploying pretrained models), PartyRock (no-code playground built on Bedrock), and Amazon Q (enterprise generative assistant). Advantages of AWS services: accessibility, lower barrier to entry, efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and faster time to market.

Week 3 · D3: Foundation Model Applications (part 1)
D3 · 28%
Design considerations and foundation model selection
Selection criteria for pretrained models: cost, modality, latency, multilingual capability, size, complexity, customization, and input/output length. Effect of inference parameters on responses: temperature and input/output length.
D3 · 28%
RAG, vector databases, and agents
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and its enterprise applications with Amazon Bedrock knowledge bases. AWS services for storing embeddings: Amazon OpenSearch Service, Aurora, Neptune, DocumentDB, and RDS for PostgreSQL. Cost trade-offs between pre-training, fine-tuning, in-context learning, and RAG. The role of Agents for Amazon Bedrock in multi-step tasks.
D3 · 28%
Prompt engineering
Concepts: context, instruction, negative prompts, and the model's latent space. Techniques: chain-of-thought templates, zero-shot, single-shot, and few-shot. Benefits and best practices: improving response quality, experimentation, guardrails, specificity, and conciseness. Risks: exposure, poisoning, hijacking, and jailbreaking.

Day 21

Week 4 · D3 (part 2) + D4 + D5
D3 · 28%
Training, fine-tuning, and evaluating foundation models
Key training elements: pre-training, fine-tuning, and continued pre-training. Fine-tuning methods: instruction tuning, domain adaptation, and transfer learning. Data preparation: curation, governance, size, labeling, representativeness, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). Evaluation: human evaluation versus benchmark datasets. Metrics: ROUGE, BLEU, and BERTScore.
D4 · 14%
Guidelines for responsible AI
Characteristics: bias, fairness, inclusivity, robustness, safety, and veracity. Guardrails for Amazon Bedrock. Legal risks of generative AI: intellectual property infringement, biased outputs, loss of trust, and hallucinations. Dataset characteristics: inclusivity, curated and balanced data. Bias detection tools: SageMaker Clarify, Model Monitor, and Amazon A2I.
D4 · 14%
Transparency and explainability
Differences between transparent and explainable models versus black-box models. Tools: SageMaker Model Cards, open-source models, data, and licensing. The trade-off between security and model transparency. Human-centered design principles for explainable AI.
D5 · 14%
Securing AI systems
Services for protecting AI systems: IAM roles, policies, and permissions, encryption with AWS KMS, Amazon Macie, and AWS PrivateLink, all under the shared responsibility model. Source citation and data lineage. Secure data engineering practices: data quality, privacy-enhancing technologies, access control, and data integrity. Security considerations: threat detection, prompt injection, and encryption at rest and in transit.
D5 · 14%
Governance and regulatory compliance
Compliance standards: ISO, System and Organization Controls (SOC), and algorithmic accountability laws. AWS services: Config, Inspector, Audit Manager, Artifact, CloudTrail, and Trusted Advisor. Data governance strategies: logging, residency, oversight, retention, and lifecycles. Frameworks such as the Generative AI Security Scoping Matrix.

Day 30 · AIF-C01 Exam
90 minutes · 65 questions (50 scored + 15 unscored) · Minimum score 700/1000
Includes case-study style questions: a business scenario with two or more related questions. Read the full scenario once and identify the problem, the constraints, and the type of AI needed before answering.
Don't study new content in these last two days. The brain consolidates better with light review and rest than with last-minute new reading.

Classic trap: if the scenario mentions "predicting a continuous numeric value" (price, temperature, demand) it's regression, not classification. If it mentions "predefined categories" (spam or not spam) it's classification.

Techniques

Classic trap: "improve response quality without retraining the model" always points to prompt engineering, not fine-tuning or RAG, unless the scenario mentions external data.
If the problem requires multi-step math or logical reasoning, chain-of-thought is usually the right technique.
Security risk in prompt engineering
Concepts
JailbreakPrompt injection / hijackingExposurePoisoning
Exam keywords
user tricks the modelhidden instructions in input datamodel reveals system promptmanipulated training data
Classic trap: jailbreaking and prompt injection are frequently confused. Jailbreaking is when the attacker tries to get the model to break its own rules. Prompt injection is malicious instructions embedded inside content the model processes, such as a document.
Bedrock Guardrails helps mitigate several of these risks by filtering the model's input and output content.
Foundation model evaluation metric
Metrics
ROUGEBLEUBERTScoreHuman evaluation
Exam keywords
evaluate summary qualityevaluate translation qualityevaluate semantic similarity
Classic trap: ROUGE and BLEU are both n-gram based but for different tasks. ROUGE evaluates summaries and is recall-oriented (how much of the reference text shows up in the summary). BLEU evaluates translations and is precision-oriented (how much of the generated text matches the reference).
If the scenario asks you to measure "meaning" rather than "exact words," BERTScore is the answer because it uses semantic embeddings instead of n-gram matching.

Classic trap: Clarify detects bias in the model globally, before and after deployment. Model Monitor detects when production data drifts from training data. A2I steps in on individual predictions.
"Before launching the model, verify it doesn't discriminate by demographic group" points to Clarify. "After launch, detect whether the data distribution changed" points to Model Monitor.

Classic trap: Config tracks resource configuration over time. CloudTrail tracks API calls, meaning who did what. Both are audit-related but they're not the same thing.

AWS AI/ML Service Catalog · Quick Reference

Filter by domain to review only the services you need each week. Learn each one's "job title" before digging into technical detail.

Amazon SageMaker
Full platform for building, training, tuning, and deploying your own machine learning models. The central service of Domain 1.
Amazon Rekognition
Analyzes image and video content: objects, faces, scenes, activities, and text in images.
vs Textract: Rekognition analyzes WHAT is in the image. Textract extracts TEXT and structure.
Amazon Textract
Extracts text, tables, and forms from scanned documents and PDFs, detecting the document's structure.
Typical scenario: "automate data extraction from invoices or contracts."
Amazon Transcribe
Converts speech to text. Automatic captions, call transcription, audio and video accessibility.
Amazon Translate
Translates text between languages at scale, no linguists needed. Content localization and multilingual applications.
Doesn't handle audio, text to text only. For audio: Transcribe first, then Translate.
Amazon Polly
Converts text to speech with natural voices in multiple languages. Accessibility, audio content, IVR.
Polly is text to audio. Transcribe is audio to text: the opposite.
Amazon Lex
Builds chatbots and voice interfaces with natural language understanding (NLU), the same technology behind Alexa.
vs Kendra: Lex is CONVERSATION with dialogue flows. Kendra is SEARCH over documents.
Amazon Kendra
Intelligent enterprise search that indexes internal documents and answers questions in natural language.
vs Bedrock: Kendra RETRIEVES existing information. Bedrock GENERATES new information.
Amazon Comprehend
NLP for entity extraction, sentiment analysis, key phrases, and PII detection in text at scale.
vs Macie: Comprehend analyzes PII in text in real time. Macie detects PII in data stored in S3.
Amazon Personalize
Personalized recommendation engine, the same technology Amazon.com uses, for retail, media, and streaming.
Amazon Forecast
Time-series forecasting for inventory demand, energy consumption, web traffic, and revenue.
Amazon Fraud Detector
Real-time fraud detection with machine learning, for fraudulent accounts and suspicious transactions.
Amazon Bedrock
Managed API for accessing multiple foundation models from different providers. The most-mentioned service on the exam.
SageMaker JumpStart
Hub of pretrained models with fine-tuning and deployment options inside SageMaker.
PartyRock
No-code playground on top of Amazon Bedrock for building generative apps in minutes.
For quick experimentation, not production.
Amazon Q
Enterprise generative assistant: Amazon Q Business for company data, Amazon Q Developer for code.
Agents para Amazon Bedrock
Orchestrate multi-step tasks: the model decides which tools or APIs to invoke to accomplish a goal.
Knowledge Bases para Amazon Bedrock
Managed RAG: connects a foundation model to your own data sources through a vector store.
Amazon OpenSearch Service
Search and analytics engine that also works as a vector store for embeddings in RAG architectures.
SageMaker Clarify
Detects bias in data and models, and explains predictions using Partial Dependence Plots.
SageMaker Model Monitor
Detects data drift and model quality degradation once in production.
Amazon A2I
Adds human review for low-confidence, high-impact predictions: medical documents, content moderation, compliance.
SageMaker Model Cards
Document a model's purpose, limitations, training data, and metrics for transparency and governance.
Bedrock Guardrails
Configurable guardrails for filtering foundation models' input and output content.
AWS IAM
Roles, policies, and permissions to control who can invoke and manage SageMaker and Bedrock resources.
AWS KMS
Encryption at rest for training data, models, and inference artifacts.
AWS PrivateLink
Private access to AI service endpoints without traversing the public internet.
Amazon Macie
Detects and classifies sensitive information (PII) stored in Amazon S3 buckets.
AWS Config + CloudTrail
Config tracks resource configuration over time. CloudTrail logs every API call: who did what and when.
AWS Audit Manager + Artifact
Audit Manager continuously collects compliance evidence. Artifact delivers ready-made compliance reports (SOC, ISO) for download.
AWS Trusted Advisor
Automated best-practice recommendations for cost, security, performance, and resilience.
